# Changelog

## 1.4.0 — October 29, 2026

Surround to your receiver as it was mixed, picture choices made for the graphics card you have, and seeking that follows the video again.

- **Surround as it was mixed.** With an AV receiver or soundbar on HDMI as Windows' sound output, Dolby Atmos (TrueHD and Dolby Digital Plus), DTS:X and the rest now go to it untouched, so it plays their height channels; before, they reached it flattened to 7.1. Exo Cinema asks Windows which formats your output takes and sends only those, and the receiver's own volume sets the level, so the volume slider steps aside meanwhile. It stays off while another app plays through the same output, while a game runs and during an Exo voice call, and a call or a game starting mid-film gets the sound back in two seconds. Headphones, speakers and USB or Bluetooth sound play as before. _Checked in code only; needs an AV receiver. On this PC's headphones and monitor nothing passes through and the player's sound starts as before._
- **The graphics card you have.** The player now asks Direct3D about the card it draws on, instead of going by its name: whether it's built in, and which video formats it decodes itself. On a laptop with two graphics chips it draws on the faster one while plugged in, so its GeForce or Radeon gets High quality and the upscalers. Intel's built-in Arc graphics and AMD's built-in RX Vega were taken for graphics cards, and a Radeon PRO for built-in graphics; each now gets what it can run. Best picks a stream above 1080p only when your card decodes it itself, so a card without AV1 or HEVC decoding no longer starts a 4K stream it would decode in software. A choice made in Windows' Settings › Graphics still wins. _Verified: on an RTX 3070 the player drew on the card it read, decoding AV1 and the rest itself, with RTX upscaling at 1.5x and no dropped frames; and in the new tests, on 33 simulated PCs from a GT 710 to an RTX 5090, built-in graphics, handhelds, hybrid laptops and PCs with two makers' cards._
- **Seeking follows the video again.** In 1.3.0, once the anime upscaler had been built in the background, the seek bar and the time could stop following the video for as long as it had already played. They follow it again, and the hover pictures let go of their connection before a jump. A stream whose host can't jump within it is passed over for the next one that can; when none can, a jump goes as far as what's loaded. _Verified: on a test server, a jump to 7:30 after the player restarted landed in 250 ms with the bar within 0.1 s of the video, and Jellyfin heard the new position._
- **Updates from Exo's own copy.** New versions download from Exo's copy of each release on Cloudflare; GitHub sends one only in the minutes before its copy is there. PRIVACY says so. _Checked in code only._
- **Up to date.** libmpv's October 29 build (mpv v0.41.0-1349, FFmpeg N-128186); the Windows App SDK, the WebView2 SDK and .NET 10.0.14 were already the newest. _Checked: no newer stable version of any other part._
## 1.3.0 — October 28, 2026

Pictures on the seek bar, scrubbing that keeps up, the best stream and tracks picked by their details, and anime redrawn by ArtCNN.

- **See where you'd jump.** Hover the seek bar and a picture of that moment shows above it, with the time: your Jellyfin server's own previews when it made them (trickplay), otherwise drawn on the spot from the nearest keyframe by a second player Exo Cinema keeps hidden, in about 15 ms a picture. It closes a minute after you stop hovering, and when you leave the player. Not for a stream the server converts, where every picture would start another conversion. _Verified: on screen with a title that has server previews, one that doesn't and a stream add-on's title; a fast sweep brought back 32 pictures._
- **Scrubbing that keeps up.** Dragging fast left the time and its glass trailing the pointer, and seeks sent in a row could make the bar jump back to an earlier one. The time and the picture now move with the pointer at the screen's own pace, and the bar holds where you sent it until the video lands there. _Verified: across 120 frames of a fast drag the bar and the time always agreed, and six quick presses of L moved the bar only forward._
- **Ask lists every stream.** With Settings › Playback › Source on Ask, a title from a stream add-on now lists the add-on's streams, best first, each with what its name says: 4K, Dolby Vision, HEVC, remux or web, size, bitrate and sound. It used to play without asking, since the server holds such a title once. _Verified: on screen with a test add-on's five streams; picking one played that stream._
- **The best stream, picked by its details.** On Best, Exo Cinema ranks what your add-on returns, after its own filters and sort. First comes a stream that can start now (the debrid service already holds it), never a camera copy, and one your connection has carried smoothly before. Then come the most detail your PC is built for, a picture your graphics card decodes, the cleanest source, HDR, the fullest sound and the most seeders, and the add-on's own order last. _Verified: a test add-on's cached 4K WEB-DL came first, its uncached remux last, and its camera copy below every real release._
- **The right audio and subtitles.** A video starts on the best recording in the language you'd expect: lossless before lossy, more channels before fewer, never a commentary track, and Japanese for anime. When the audio isn't in your language you get full subtitles in yours (styled ones first, as anime's are, never signs only); for a dub, only its signs and songs. What you pick yourself still wins, for the rest of the show. _Verified on sample track lists: an anime's Japanese FLAC and full English subtitles over its English default and signs track; an English film's TrueHD Atmos with forced subtitles only; untagged audio left alone._
- **Anime, sharper.** Anime is redrawn by ArtCNN, a neural network trained on drawn art: lines come out crisp and backgrounds keep their texture. 720p, 1080p and 1440p episodes are drawn at up to 4K and shrunk back to your screen. Exo Cinema builds ArtCNN for your graphics card once, in the background (about 40 seconds, never while a game runs), and uses the driver's upscaler until then. Chosen on a picture bench against Anime4K and NVIDIA's upscaler, where it came closest to the clean original on every test. _Verified on an RTX 3070: 24% of the GPU and no dropped frames; after the background build the next anime started in 116 ms._
- **Sharper pictures.** High quality now shrinks a bigger picture with Catmull-Rom instead of mpv's softer default. NVIDIA's RTX Video Super Resolution now only enlarges a video smaller than your screen: drawn at twice the size and shrunk back, a full-size picture came out softer than without it. _Verified: on the picture bench, and a 720p film enlarged 1.5x on screen._
- **Starts sooner.** The player keeps the shaders it builds for your graphics card instead of building them again at every start. _Verified: a heavy set of shaders went from 3 seconds to 0.06._
- **Exo's own subtitles.** Plain subtitles (SRT, WebVTT) are drawn in Inter, Exo's type, with a dark edge and a soft shadow; styled ones keep their own look and fonts. _Verified: on screen._
- **The poster in Windows.** Windows' media controls and Exo Launcher's mini player now show the poster of what's playing (the show's, for an episode). It was missing. _Verified: read back through Windows' media session as a JPEG._
- **Full screen stays put.** Going to or from full screen never makes Exo Cinema the active window, in front of your game or behind it, and a full-screen window can't be dragged or snapped by its top edge. _Verified: full-screen changes in the background left the active window where it was._
- **Glass that fits.** A title that changes length, or a menu filling in, resizes its glass at once. _Verified: on screen._
- **Reduce motion from the start.** The first run's Look step has the Reduce motion switch too, and Setup's own animations play again. _Checked in code only._
- **Installed apps.** Windows' Installed apps lists Exo as the publisher, with links to getexo.app and the Exo Discord, and Exo Cinema keeps its version there right. _Checked in code only; needs an installed copy._
- **Up to date.** Every part was already the newest: libmpv's October 28 build, .NET 10.0.14, the Windows App SDK and the WebView2 SDK. ArtCNN (MIT) joins the third-party notices. _Checked: no newer stable version of any part._

## 1.2.0 — October 28, 2026

Updates offered as Exo Cinema opens, your add-on link back in reach, a Reduce motion switch of its own, and a window that stays behind your game.

- **Updates when you open it.** Exo Cinema looks for a new version once, as it opens, and offers it on a sheet: "Update available", the version and **Update now**, which downloads it with its progress, checks it and installs it; the new version opens and What's New shows what changed. A version that comes out while Exo Cinema is open waits for the next open, so nothing interrupts a film; the twelve-hour check is gone. A failed update says why, with **Copy logs**, **Try again** and **Not now**; one that didn't take is offered with **Not now** from the start, so nobody is held in a loop. The check asks Exo's update address (`updates.getexo.app`) first and GitHub when it doesn't answer, so a renamed account can't strand an installed Exo Cinema. _Verified: a test copy marked 1.0.0 was offered 1.1.0 through updates.getexo.app and downloaded and checked its 62.9 MB Setup in about 2 seconds; the retry state, Copy logs and Not now on screen. Installing and reopening: checked in code only, as test copies never run Setup._
- **Sheets hold the window.** While What's New or the update sheet is up, nothing behind it scrolls or reacts to keys, and a press on the dim does nothing; the window's own buttons stay usable on top. _Verified: on screen and with keys (Ctrl+K and Backspace did nothing behind it)._
- **Your add-on link, when you need it.** Settings › Playback › Stream addon showed a saved link as dots even to you, with no way to read or copy it. It now shows in full while you're in the field and as dots otherwise (so it stays off screenshots and streams), and **Copy** copies it without showing it. _Verified: on screen._
- **Reduce motion, your way.** Settings › Look has a Reduce motion switch, as Exo Launcher has. The first start takes it from Windows' animation effects; after that only the switch decides, and it always shows what's in effect. Windows' setting no longer quietly stills Exo Cinema with the switch Off. _Verified: with Windows' setting emulated both ways, the first start and the switch kept afterwards._
- **Stays behind your game.** Exo Cinema came to the front by itself in two ways: a sheet focusing itself as a window opened in the background, and a change to or from full screen (an episode ending while you play) with another window in use. Focus the page moves by itself now waits for you to click into Exo Cinema, and a full-screen change waits until it's the active window. _Verified: a background start with the update sheet, and a background full-screen request, left the window in front where it was._
- **Up to date.** libmpv is zhongfly's LGPL build of October 28 (mpv v0.41.0-1346-gdc4b285c9, FFmpeg N-128155-g8e362b7b5); every other part was already the newest. _Verified: 720p with RTX Super Resolution, and 4K60 Dolby Vision with no dropped frames, on the release build._

## 1.1.0 — October 28, 2026

RTX upscaling without the ripple, an app that rests when you're not using it, pins that survive updates, and Exo Launcher's notices and motion.

- **RTX upscaling without the ripple.** RTX Video Super Resolution was sized from the screen's height, not the window's: in a maximized window (shorter than the screen) or for a widescreen film (1920x800 on a 1080p screen went up to 2592x1080), mpv then shrank the upscaled picture back by a sliver, and that made the whole video ripple and look wavy. It now upscales to exactly the size the video is shown at, so mpv doesn't scale it again, and sizes itself again when the window is resized or goes full screen. _Verified: a 720p clip in a 1904x1072 window came out of the upscaler at exactly 1904x1072, and re-fitted to 1584x890 after a resize to 1584x892._
- **Next to nothing in the background.** The player's progress bar redrew on every frame forever, even with no video open: about 6% of a CPU core at idle, minimized or behind a game. It now draws only while it's on screen and moving, and moves on transforms instead of relaying the page out; the Skip and Up Next check runs only while a video is open; a minimized Exo Cinema stops drawing its page and gives memory back; and its spinners pause while another window is in front. _Verified, on this PC (the app plus its WebView2 processes): idle on Home 5.7% of a core to 0.5%; idle after use 5.9% to 0.05%; minimized 6.2% to 0.08%, using 237 MB instead of 577 MB; playing 720p with RTX upscaling 40.6% to 18.4%. The page's memory stayed flat over three rounds of every page and the player._
- **Pins that stay.** Windows ties a Start or taskbar pin to an app's ID. 1.0.8 put Exo Cinema's own ID on its shortcuts, which changed it and unpinned Exo Cinema; Setup now writes that ID on the shortcuts as it makes them, so it never changes again. If 1.0.8 unpinned it, pin it once more and it stays. _Verified: a test install and a test update over it both kept the shortcut's ID._
- **Notices like Exo Launcher's.** Messages show at the top right under the bar, stack, stay 4.5 seconds and wait while the pointer is on them. _Verified: on screen._
- **Motion like Exo Launcher's.** Every time and curve now comes from Exo Launcher's tokens: the boot, fades and the progress fill were snapped to them; menus grow from their button and sheets settle in; the light a press makes on glass is drawn once and only fades and grows (it repainted the glass on every frame of a press); and under Windows' reduce motion nothing loops. _Checked in code; the press light on screen._
- **Up to date.** The Windows SDK build tools are on 10.0.28000.2957; .NET 10.0.14, libmpv (mpv v0.41.0-1320) and every other package were already the newest. _Checked in code only._

## 1.0.8 — October 28, 2026

Exo Cinema joins Windows' media controls.

- **Media keys and Windows' media controls.** While the player is open, Exo Cinema is a media session in Windows: the keyboard's play, pause and next keys work with another window in front, and Windows' media controls show the title, the show and episode (or the year) and the poster, with play and pause, a timeline to seek on, 10 seconds back and forward, and Next when a next episode follows. Closing the player ends the session, so the media keys go back to your music. _Verified: end-to-end through Windows' media-session API; pause, play, seek and Next each reached the player, an episode showed its show, number and poster, and closing the player ended the session._
- **In Exo Launcher's Now Playing.** Exo Launcher reads Windows' media sessions, so it can show and steer Exo Cinema like its other sources (app ID `UhhErix.ExoCinema`). _Checked in code only; needs a matching Exo Launcher._
- **Its own name and icon.** Exo Cinema stamps its app ID on its own Start menu and desktop shortcuts, so Windows names it "Exo Cinema" with its icon in the media controls, and a pinned taskbar icon stays one icon. _Verified: end-to-end on a test shortcut with the release build; other shortcuts are left alone._
- **Opens during a driver update.** While a graphics driver installs, its Vulkan loader is gone for a minute or two and mpv can't load; Exo Cinema opened to an empty window then. Now it opens as usual, and the player starts the next time it's needed (or Play says to try again once the driver is done). _Verified: found during an NVIDIA driver install, when the release build couldn't load mpv; checked in code only since, as the install had finished._

## 1.0.5 — October 28, 2026

Settings with one right answer are now just done.

- **The picture picks itself.** Settings > Playback > Picture is gone. Exo Cinema draws High quality (mpv's high-quality scalers, with RTX Video Super Resolution where it's on) on a dedicated graphics card on mains power, and Balanced on integrated graphics and on battery. A video that drops a frame a second for a minute goes Balanced for the rest of it; the next one starts on High again. The player's readings (I) say which. _Verified: on an RTX 3070, a 720p clip played on High with RTX Super Resolution; made to drop 62 frames in a minute, it went Balanced and the upscaler stepped aside; the next video started on High; a minimized window dropped none. On battery: checked in code only, as the test PC is a desktop._
- **Hardware decoding, always.** Its switch is gone: video is decoded on the graphics card, and mpv falls back to software for anything the card can't decode, as it did with the switch on. _Verified: Direct3D 11 decoding on the test clip._
- **First run:** the Playback step now sets Source and Play next episode.

## 1.0.4 — October 28, 2026

AI upscaling on RTX cards, and updates that wait for the folder.

- **AI upscaling on RTX cards.** A video smaller than the screen (720p on a 1080p screen, 1080p on a 1440p or 4K one) is enlarged by NVIDIA's RTX Video Super Resolution as it plays, through mpv's Direct3D 11 video processor, as Edge and Chrome ask for it: sharper edges and finer detail than any scaler can draw. It takes Picture on High quality, SDR video decoded on the card, and RTX Video Super Resolution on in Exo Launcher's PC > Display (or NVIDIA's own settings); Exo Cinema reads that switch and never changes it. With it off, mpv's own high-quality scalers enlarge the picture as before. _Verified: on an RTX 3070, the same frame scaled 2x had four times the edge detail with it; 720p played upscaled to 1080p with no frames dropped._
- **Games come first.** While Exo Launcher has a game running, the upscaler steps aside so the game keeps the graphics card, and comes back when the game ends. _Verified: with Exo Launcher's game signal set and cleared._
- **Updates that wait.** An update stopped at "Exo Cinema's folder is in use" when one of the app's WebView2 helpers was still closing; Setup now tries again for up to 20 seconds. Found by Exo Browser, whose updates failed the same way. _Verified: an update with the app folder held for 6 seconds finished in 9._

## 1.0.3 — October 28, 2026

The best version of every title, or your pick.

## 1.0.0 — October 27, 2026

The first release: your Jellyfin library in Exo's look, with a native player.

- **Your Jellyfin library.** Sign in with your server's address, user name and password, or with Quick Connect where the server offers it. Home shows Continue Watching, Next Up and what's new in each library; the Bar holds your libraries; a show's page lists its seasons and episodes; Ctrl+K searches the whole server. _Verified: end-to-end; Quick Connect checked in code only._
